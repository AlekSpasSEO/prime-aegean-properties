Step 02 / Budget & prepare · 3 min read

How to budget for buying a home in Greece

Build a budget that covers the purchase, professional fees and the first months of ownership.

Ask for a written estimate before you offer

Start with your total budget and set aside the money you’ll need to complete the purchase and get the home ready. Ask the notary which transaction tax applies, what value it will be assessed on and when it is due. Where property transfer tax applies, the buyer is responsible for paying it.

Get separate estimates for the notary, registration, lawyer and engineer. Ask the agent whether a buyer’s fee applies and confirm the amount and payment terms in writing. If you’re buying from abroad, include any translation, currency conversion and bank transfer costs.

Set up your tax and payment paperwork

Confirm that you have a Greek tax identification number (AFM). If you need one, Greece’s tax authority, AADE, provides an application through myAADE. Ask your advisers what identification and documents they need from you, especially if you’ll be abroad around the signing date.

If you need a mortgage, ask your lender what it can confirm now and what depends on a valuation or the property documents. If you’ll pay from abroad, check bank transfer limits and lead times before you agree to a payment date.

Budget for the first year

Ask for recent utility bills and any shared building charges. Make room for insurance, repairs and ongoing costs such as annual property tax (ENFIA), where applicable.

Keep three totals in your notes for each home you’re comparing. Write down the price, the amount you expect to need to complete the purchase and the amount you want to keep for work and running costs.
